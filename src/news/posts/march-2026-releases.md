Three days of releases across the Mac and iPad lines. The [MacBook Neo](/news/macbook-neo-launch/) and the [iPhone 17e](/news/iphone-17e-launch/) landed in the same stretch, and are covered separately.

### M5 Pro and M5 Max — 3 March

The [MacBook Pro 14″](/devices/mac/macbook-pro-14/) and [16″](/devices/mac/macbook-pro-16/) gained the [M5 Pro](/chips/m5/pro/) and [M5 Max](/chips/m5/max/), both manufactured on TSMC's 3nm node, with 16 Neural Engine cores and 61 TOPS.

The [18c CPU / 32c GPU M5 Max](/chips/m5/max/18-32/) is the configuration to watch: a 384-bit bus at 460 GB/s and a single 36 GB memory option, against 512-bit, 614 GB/s and up to 128 GB on the [18c CPU / 40c GPU](/chips/m5/max/18-40/). Both chips would [reach the Mac mini and Mac Studio six months later](/news/mac-mini-mac-studio-refresh/).

The same day, the [MacBook Air 13″](/devices/mac/macbook-air-13/) picked up a [10c CPU / 8c GPU M5](/chips/m5/base/10-8/) — two GPU cores down on the [10c CPU / 10c GPU](/chips/m5/base/10-10/) part in the [15″](/devices/mac/macbook-air-15/), though both run at 153.6 GB/s with the same 16, 24 and 32 GB options. No other device uses it.

### M4 iPad Air — 2 March

The [iPad Air 11″](/devices/ipad/air/11/) and [13″](/devices/ipad/air/13/) moved to the [8c CPU / 9c GPU M4](/chips/m4/base/8-9/).
